# Chemistry

## Atomic Structure

An atom consists of a nucleus containing protons and neutrons, surrounded by electrons in orbitals. The atomic number is the number of protons in the nucleus and defines the element. The mass number is the total number of protons and neutrons. Isotopes are atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons.

Electrons occupy energy levels or shells. The first shell holds a maximum of 2 electrons, the second holds 8, and the third holds 18. Electrons fill the lowest available energy level first, following the Aufbau principle. The outermost shell electrons are called valence electrons and determine chemical behavior.

## The Periodic Table

The periodic table arranges elements by increasing atomic number in rows called periods and columns called groups. Elements in the same group have the same number of valence electrons and similar chemical properties. Metals are on the left and center, nonmetals on the right, and metalloids along the staircase boundary.

Group 1 elements (alkali metals) have one valence electron and are highly reactive. Group 17 (halogens) have seven valence electrons and readily gain one electron. Group 18 (noble gases) have full outer shells and are chemically inert.

Electronegativity increases across a period (left to right) and decreases down a group. Atomic radius decreases across a period and increases down a group. Ionization energy increases across a period and decreases down a group.

## Chemical Bonding

Ionic bonds form when electrons are transferred from a metal to a nonmetal, creating oppositely charged ions that attract. Covalent bonds form when two nonmetals share electrons. Polar covalent bonds occur when electrons are shared unequally due to electronegativity differences.

Metallic bonding involves a sea of delocalized electrons surrounding positive metal ions, which explains the conductivity and malleability of metals. Hydrogen bonding is a strong intermolecular force between molecules containing H bonded to N, O, or F.

## Chemical Reactions

A chemical reaction breaks old bonds and forms new ones. The law of conservation of mass states that mass is neither created nor destroyed. A balanced equation has equal numbers of each atom on both sides.

Synthesis reactions: A + B → AB. Decomposition reactions: AB → A + B. Single replacement: A + BC → AC + B. Double replacement: AB + CD → AD + CB. Combustion: fuel + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O.

## Acids and Bases

Acids donate protons (H⁺ ions) in solution. Bases accept protons or donate hydroxide ions (OH⁻). The pH scale runs from 0 to 14. A pH below 7 is acidic, 7 is neutral, above 7 is basic. Each unit on the pH scale represents a tenfold change in hydrogen ion concentration.

Strong acids completely dissociate in water: HCl, HNO₃, H₂SO₄. Strong bases also fully dissociate: NaOH, KOH. Weak acids and bases only partially dissociate. Buffers resist changes in pH by neutralizing added acids or bases.

## Thermochemistry

Exothermic reactions release heat energy (negative ΔH). Endothermic reactions absorb heat (positive ΔH). Enthalpy (H) measures heat content at constant pressure. Hess's Law states that the total enthalpy change is independent of the reaction pathway.

Activation energy is the minimum energy needed for a reaction to occur. Catalysts lower activation energy without being consumed. Enzymes are biological catalysts.

## Stoichiometry

The mole is the SI unit for amount of substance: 6.022 × 10²³ particles (Avogadro's number). Molar mass is the mass of one mole in grams, equal numerically to atomic mass. Stoichiometry uses mole ratios from balanced equations to calculate reactant and product quantities.

Percent yield = (actual yield / theoretical yield) × 100. Limiting reagent is the reactant that runs out first and determines the maximum product formed.

## Gases

Boyle's Law: pressure and volume are inversely proportional at constant temperature (P₁V₁ = P₂V₂). Charles's Law: volume and temperature are directly proportional at constant pressure (V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂). The Ideal Gas Law: PV = nRT where R = 8.314 J/mol·K.

Standard Temperature and Pressure (STP) is 0°C and 1 atm. At STP, one mole of any ideal gas occupies 22.4 liters.
